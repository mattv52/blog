I slept pretty well last night.

Waking up this morning, the tent wasn't wet from dew for only like the 3rd time this trip. Which was lovely.

I packed up and set off before any of the school kids were out of their tents.

The morning was pretty calm, just along the last bit of the forest before I got to Kuitpo Forrest, which contains a big tree climb activity.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-32/1000035171.jpg" | relative_url }})

The best thing about these forest areas are that there is so much shade I don't need to have my hat on

I walked up past the tree climb and through a less structured, more natural area. It's quite interesting the difference in vibes when really it's all just trees

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-32/1000035174.jpg" | relative_url }})

I had walked this section with Phil previously while he was completing it. From here through to mount compass (where I was planning to finish today). I'm not sure if it's because I'd done it before, because I'm close to the end or a combination of both, but I felt pretty disengaged today. Even looking back at the pictures I took to write this I was forgetting which day was which.

Leaving Kuitpo Forest, there's a big pine plantation. This particular field has been harvested. The sky was also starting to turn, grey clouds rolling in

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-32/1000035175.jpg" | relative_url }})

The clouds passed over with only the lightest of sprinkles, I didn't even get my rain gear out.

I went into a nice conservation park along the road, which was very nice and shady

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-32/1000035178.jpg" | relative_url }})

The trail goes along next to mount magnificent. Last time I came through here with Phil, we summited it so I didn't feel the need to do it again.

Mount Magnificent can be seen on the left side

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-32/1000035181.jpg" | relative_url }})

I bumped into another couple out on a walk through the trail too. They are retirees, who have spent the last several years setting up a trail in the Willunga basin.

I walked with them for about 45 minutes until they got to their car. They had parked one at either end of a small section they were scouting for a bush walking club they're part of.

We walked past a river section that was flowing pretty quick

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-32/1000035185.jpg" | relative_url }})

There was another stretch of proper road walking again today, with a fair bit of traffic. It was also raining now and I had my umbrella up and rain coat on.

It pretty much rained non-stop from here til I got to camp.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-32/1000035187.jpg" | relative_url }})

The rain didn't bother me much here, as I could just have the umbrella up. But later I was going through much more dense trees and had to put it away for a bit.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-32/1000035188.jpg" | relative_url }})

Looking at that picture it looks lovely and sunny. It wasn't. It was pretty miserable.

I made it into the mount compass caravan park around 4, and had a lot of time to chill. I'm just in my tent here, not a cabin.

Since it just stopped raining, I set my tent up then kinda spread my stuff out all over the camp kitchen with the heater running to try dry out my stuff.

I rang ahead while it was raining and asked if I could hire a towel. Knowing id be keen for a hot shower when I got there. The guy said they don't normally, but he can do that. Also said I'm free to pick anywhere to camp when I got here instead of being cast off into the unpowered site corner. So I've got prime position right next to the kitchen and bathrooms.

The place has felt pretty deserted all afternoon. I guess people are out for the day/dinner?

As I got into bed a few people returned and I can hear several TVs playing in caravans.

Tomorrow will be a “shorter” day, as I head to Myponga, which I'm thankful for. I don't have many left now, and this will serve as a nice break from the last couple big 40k days, and before the next couple big days along the coast.
