**Stats Summary**

Mount Arden South Camp Site to Quorn

Running Total

232.3km. 2d 19h 20m. 6703m ⬆️ 6636m ⬇️

During the night, one of the pegs holding my tent in place came out, causing one edge of it to collapse. I think it was a combination of rain and wind. My tent is held up by the tension from the pegs and the hiking poles in the middle. So when the pegs come out, it becomes unstable and can all fall over quite easily.

So in a mad rush, in the rain, barefoot, with just my raincoat and underwear on, I went around and quickly hammered all my pegs in further. I think I woke Phil up and he got out and did the same just to be safe.

Luckily that's the only time so far, and hopefully it's the last.

Because there was another long day planned, we got up early. Not quite as early as yesterday because the trail didn't look quite as friendly in the dark, and it was going to be raining.

From when we woke up to as we left, it was dry. Then as soon as we begin walking the rain came down. Which was great for us, meant our tents and gear was dry in our bags.

The morning was lots of creek following. Slow descent down the rest of Mount Arden. We were very wet by the time the sun came up, but in good spirits looking forward to our chicken parmis and warm showers in Quorn.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000034097.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000034096.jpg" | relative_url }})

We followed the dry (now trickling from all the rain) river bed the whole way down. It was mostly large rocks, which are much easier to manoeuvre through, just slow. And with the rain, it made everything that little bit more slippery.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000033946.jpg" | relative_url }})

Along the way, we found another distance marker. The 200km point. Another great photo opportunity

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000034095.jpg" | relative_url }})

Down the river, there was a huge drop off. What would be a sizeable waterfall if the river was flowing. And in the past, flow it seems to have, washing away the ground at the bottom making it an even larger drop. We were up the top and had to find a way down. The rain was making the scramble down not seem possible/safe

I'd estimate it to be about 5m, but here is me for scale.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000034094.jpg" | relative_url }})

After some looking, I found a bit to the side that seemed to be a bit nicer, but still very sketchy. I threw my pole down and took off my pack, then descended rock climbing style. It was very unideal, but I made it safely down. Now LuckiePhil's turn. He passed down our packs then came down himself. The first part he grabbed ripped right off the wall and I had to jump out the way. Eventually he made his was down too. It was probably the hardest single part of the trail so far

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000033950.jpg" | relative_url }})

When we finally reached the bottom of the hill, there was a small shelter, then a long stretch of flat countryside walking. We decided to have an early lunch under the shelter to get out of the rain.

It was also the first time we'd got reception today, so we tried to book our hotel in Quorn. But unfortunately, there were nothing available. Thinking it was a mistake and feeling our parmi’s slip away, we asked Phil's wife back home to phone around and see. Eventually she got back to us. There was no for us room at the inn. And we weren't going to be sleeping in a manger.

Luckily, just as we got reception, my Uncle and Aunty in Port Augusta asked if they could meet us in Quorn. So we asked them if there was any chance we could crash at theirs and thankfully they said yes. We are so grateful.

Then we continued on along the countryside and found another distance marker. This time for the western most point

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000034093.jpg" | relative_url }})

Eventually we had to come back up into The Dutchmans Stern Conservation Park. Which required another long sustained push uphill. How do we always end up with the uphill towards the end of the day…

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000034092.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000033959.jpg" | relative_url }})

Eventually we made it to Dutchman's Hut. Which is a pretty good hut. Probably the nicest we've seen so far. Has flushing toilets, and electricity. Both rarities on the trail.

We just wanted somewhere out of the elements for a quick break, but I was pretty knackered by this point

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-8/1000034091.jpg" | relative_url }})

The final push from the hut into Quorn wasnt quite as flat as we'd have liked. Eventually we made it out onto the road and we're powering along. Planning to meet at Quorn for pickup.

Thankfully, my Uncle and Aunty came out and met us a couple km outside of town, where we stopped our trackers and will begin them again on Monday after a much needed rest day tomorrow. Off to Port Augusta for us now

By the end of the day, I had slipped over twice more to Phil's 1. Leaving the total for the trip to 5 for Phil, 3 for me. This has been a little friendly competition between us

While this day was shorter in both distance and time compared to yesterday, I think the worse weather and the fact it came directly after made it feel a lot harder. Definitely the hardest day on trail, and I hope there's not many harder. Wouldnt recommend trying to combo these 2 days back to back like this normally.
