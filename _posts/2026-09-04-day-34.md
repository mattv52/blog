Knowing it would be a big day today, I planned to leave at 5:30. My alarm went off and I just wanted to sleep more in my comfortable bed. So I left at 6 instead.

It couldn't quite decide if it wanted to rain or not all morning. It was super foggy and a bit misty

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035265.jpg" | relative_url }})

Not far in, I crossed the 1000km marker. I did 1000km on my tracker yesterday, but I've done a few off trail detours (like Myponga yesterday) so this is the official marker

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035273.jpg" | relative_url }})

Then it was uphill through a small conservation area with lots of over grown trees.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035276.jpg" | relative_url }})

When walking without a backpack, you would simply duck underneath and have no concerns. With this heavy large pack on, it makes that a lot harder. Something small like this I can just move up and go under, but sometimes it requires some pretty good limbo skills

Eventually I reached the top

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035279.jpg" | relative_url }})

One of the worst feelings is when you spend all this effort to reach the top, just to be sent straight back down the other side. Thankfully, this time the trail followed along the ridge up the top for a while.

At the end of the ridge, there was a steep ish downhill into Inman Valley. It was all wet and muddy, and the ground was under over grown plants so hard to see where to step. All this to say, I stepped wrong and slide down the hill a bit. Now Phil and I are tied on falls. I better not let him win.

This was the view at the top before I fell

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035285.jpg" | relative_url }})

I was hoping to get something to eat there, but it was all shut.

After the town, there was a pine forest, except this one must have just been harvested and is being replanted

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035300.jpg" | relative_url }})

Then I saw the sign of the trip. I think I need to get one of these

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035302.jpg" | relative_url }})

Not really sure why it's on this one road and there haven't been any others in much more sketchy road sections.

In a paddock, there's were like 40 kangaroos all jumpig through together. A group of them all stopped so I managed to snap a pic, but there was easily 3x this many. I'd never seen so many together before

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035306.jpg" | relative_url }})

In the same paddock, there were some more highland cows with horns

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035312.jpg" | relative_url }})

Today, I got very lost. It seems as though the farmer of this field has put up a new fence, which cut access of the trail. I think the markers on trail were updated to reflect this, but the online map hasn't been. And I was following the online map (and worn in trail of many previous years). I ended up a fair way off and in the end just had to jump 2 fences to get back to where I should be.

I got my first stretch of sand too which was exciting to start, then a massive pain to walk through

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035316.jpg" | relative_url }})

Through the sand section and I could finally see water!

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035317.jpg" | relative_url }})

Still a long way to go, but that's the coast which I'll be mostly following for the next couple days.

A little closer and I could see the bluff

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035321.jpg" | relative_url }})

At this point (just past Kings beach) I have now technically walked the whole trail as I did the section from Cape Jervis to Victor Harbor.

A lot of this final stretch is up on cliffs and through the bush

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035323.jpg" | relative_url }})

There's a beautiful view of the cliffs and looking back at the bluff

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-34/1000035327.jpg" | relative_url }})

When I finally got to camp, it was already getting dark. Most camps along the coast here are in pairs. One for walk in hikers only (no car access) and one for everyone. I chose to go to the everyone site this time because it was quite a bit cheaper. I was a little worried that it was gonna be busy and there would be some group of drunk teenagers blasting music all night. But it was empty. Like eerily so. The whole campground had not a single other person on it.

I quickly got my tent up, went to the bathroom and by the time I got out, it was pitch black. I couldn't see any water tanks easily accessible, and had enough water to last the night so figured I'd just do it in the morning. There were also mosquitos EVERYWHERE. Maybe because I was the only human around, but I think I must have squashed 100+ easily in the 30mins I was outside setting up.

It was a really really long day. I was very tired by the end and happy to be in my sleeping bag, without any mosquitos inside the tent.
