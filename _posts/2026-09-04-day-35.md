I was up pretty early this morning and planned to get going. I still hadn't filtered at water yet so went for a walk around in the morning light to find the tank and still couldn't see any. I ended up leaving without water, knowing the next camp should have some and it wasn't too much further. Luckily there was a nice sunrise this morning to boost my mood at least

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035363.jpg" | relative_url }})

It happens to also be the day of coastrek, an annual charity event along the Fleurieu coast. There's several distances, but the 50km walk started at 6am, about 3km ahead of where I camped. That means they were all rolling through as I was leaving just before 7. I think I passed about 50 people in the morning on my way down to the beach. Remember that most of the trail is 1 person wide, so it was a lot of waiting to the side and they went past.

Eventually I made it past all of them and down onto the beach. My first beach of the day

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035365.jpg" | relative_url }})

It was all going well, until suddenly the water came at me. It seems the tide is very quick changing today. This stretch of beach looks above the tide as I was approaching and as I got there, a big wave came in and the whole section went under. I managed to jump up out of the way onto the sand dune and snap a picture of where I was just standing. My footprints are now under all that water… and yes, my feet did get wet.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035369.jpg" | relative_url }})

After the beach, I climbed up the hill at the end and bam, my first sight of kangaroo island. It's super faint, but far on the horizon, it's definitely visible. (The end of the trail is Cape Jervis, which is where the ferry to Kangaroo Island goes. So seeing the island means I'm getting very close now)

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035372.jpg" | relative_url }})

The trail went up and down a fair bit today, with a few small little cove crossings spread between the longer beach sections.

At this little crossing, there were a lot of stones poking out above the river water level. It looked like a nice easy crossing so I went for it. Mid way through, the water came in and absolutely flooded if covering almost all the rocks. I was in the middle of crossing and definitely got wet feet again…

Here are 2 photos, one of before the tide came (when it looked safe to cross), and the other when the tide was in. Both of these photos are of the exact same spot, the water level just comes up super high

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035381.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035379.jpg" | relative_url }})

This final stretch along the coast isn't actually along the coast the whole time. There's a few times that it cuts back in land, up hills and through some farm land.

This was the first such instance of this

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035382.jpg" | relative_url }})

At the top of the hill, it was a lovely view back at the ocean with the rolling hills in the front

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035387.jpg" | relative_url }})

Walking this section along the top of the hills, it was sooooooo windy. Like the windiest of the whole trip, and I’ve had some windy days on a few mountains up north.

Having the full backpack on makes me like 2-3 times wider from the side profile, which acts as a sort of sail when the wind is coming side on. I was walking in a straight line with the wind coming at my right and having to lean at a 45° angle into the wind just to not be blown over. It was absolutely crazy.

At the end of this hill top section, was the path down to the beach. I knew this was coming up as I'd done it before, but always in the other direction. This is the hill that I thought might be in competition for the steepest hill on the trail.

As you approach it, there's a nice sign

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035395.jpg" | relative_url }})

Except, the fence just kinda vanishes over the edge. When looking over the edge this is what you see

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035396.jpg" | relative_url }})

Photos don't do it justice, but it's steep. So steep, that they've installed a chain link handrail to help you pull yourself up.

It was definitely the steepest descent on a hill I had to do, but I actually think the hill before Morialta Conservation Park (on Day 30) was probably the steepest ascent.

I ended up going down backwards like you would on a ladder and just going real slowly.

At the bottom was a long walk along the beach. It was very unsolid sand, my feet kept sinking into it which meant I was using different muscles in my legs to step through it. It was so very tiring.

The waves on the beach were pretty gnarly dude. There were a few small tubes starting to form too

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035403.jpg" | relative_url }})

When I finally reached the end of the beach, my legs were pretty sore. Due to how I was having to walk, my gait was kinda funky for a bit.

The ground was a bit wet and muddy and on a slope and stepped poorly and fell again… now I have more falls than Phil. It was a pretty sketchy spot to slip up too, this is where I'd have ended up if I slide down the side, instead of just falling on the path

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035408.jpg" | relative_url }})

There was a other lizard chilling on the trail. I thought this guy was a shredded snake skin when I first saw him, but no, it's another lizard.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035407.jpg" | relative_url }})

Some of the coves were quite nice and would be a great place to come have a picnic on a warm sunny day. Just would take a little effort to get to

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035409.jpg" | relative_url }})

Up the other side of the cove and it was a big climb up to the top of hill on the other side. It was a pretty long climb up. The view was pretty cool, very shruby with spots of golden wattle spread around

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035412.jpg" | relative_url }})

LuckiePhil’s son, and one of my friends, Sam, joined me for the last 2 days. He caught up to me on this climb up.

At the start of this year, there was a big fire through this whole deep creek area. It was shut for a few months and some of the trails are still inaccessible. They have had to re route the Heysen along a different trail for a section here due to the fire damage.

The remnants of the fire are still easy to see everywhere, with lots of burnt trees

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035414.jpg" | relative_url }})

Some of the plants seem to have resisted the fire quite well and are thriving now, but it gives the landscape a strange vibe

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-35/1000035416.jpg" | relative_url }})

We pushed pretty hard today so tomorrow, the last day, wouldn't be very long. After we got to camp and got set up, a storm started to roll in. It was super windy, so we used the extra pegs to secure the tent better.

While we were laying in the tent, we could hear the thunder and see the flashes of lightning. Fingers crossed no branches fall on the tent or pegs come out during the night again
