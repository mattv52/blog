So, I may or may not have failed to correctly calculate how many days before the next hotel back in Spalding (when I last had access to a power point) and my phone and power bank are basically flat. That means there aren't many photos today.

I left a bit early this morning, knowing the sooner I left, the sooner I get a shower. About 500m from the hut, there were another couple deer prancing around. Deer are basically kangaroos, except they jump on 4 legs, instead of 2. Theyre pretty strange animals.

Today was time for the infamous Burra hills. In each hut, there's a log book people sign, and in last night, lots of people who'd come the other way said how hilly the terrain was.

To start though, it was a nice walk through countryside.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-22/1000034634.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-22/1000034636.jpg" | relative_url }})

There were so many kangaroos hoping about. It was a pretty cool sight. There were also huge holes in the ground, that I assume we're wombat burrows. Didn't see any wombats though.

It wasn't long before the green hills of Burra turned up

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-22/1000034640.jpg" | relative_url }})

There were many big hills to climb, and each time, it was straight back down the other side. Some of them were quite manageable, others were super steep and took a big effort to get up.

About halfway there was a campsite, which I was bypassing to get to town. My mate from the last few days was having lunch there when I walked past and so we walked together for the final stretch into Burra.

He is a mountain goat when it comes to going up hills. He just breezes through without a break, whereas I am constantly out of breath and needing a minute.

Finally made it to the top of the final hill, and it's just all downhill into Burra now. You can see I was so exhausted, I forgot I had to look at the camera lol

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-22/1000034643.jpg" | relative_url }})

We rolled into town nice and early, around 3. We split up, he was looking for accommodation, and my parents had come up from Adelaide to hang out for the night so they got me a place already. LuckiePhil also came up with them, to join me for the next couple days and fully complete the trail. This little section was the last bit for him to complete.

Along with free accommodation, my parents brought up a fresh pair of shoes for me. My current ones had done over 600km, more than half the trail now, and had pretty much lost their cushion, so it was time. I got the exact same (same size, style and colour) ones to what I had been using. Mainly so there wouldn't be any unknown issues, but I also wanted to see how much they had worn out.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-22/1000034665.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-22/1000034666.jpg" | relative_url }})

It's a bit hard to see, but they wore through alot. Especially on the foam in the sole, but also the traction lugs.

That shower I was so looking forward to lived up to its hype. I think I i ended up spending about 20 minutes in there, however half that time was trying to get the strapping off my knee. I'd left it on since the second time I strapped it, which was Crystal Brook 6 days ago. Mainly because I was low on tape, but also because I didn't want to deal with this. It was a lot of pain...

I also have pretty distinct tan lines now too lol

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-22/1000034719.jpg" | relative_url }})

It was a pretty grueling day today. I pushed pretty hard, knowing there was a shower waiting for me in town. My knee is feeling much better now, but we'll see how it is without the strapping tomorrow
