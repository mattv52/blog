It rained basically non stop all night and through to today. It was nothing crazy, just a steady drizzle and it was forecast to end just after lunch time today which was lovely.

My clothes hadn't really fully dried, they were still partially wet, along with my sock, undies and shoes. It was really miserable getting back into wet clothes knowing it's going to be cold.

There wasn't a whole lot of visibility, from fog and rain.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034753.jpg" | relative_url }})

It didn't take long for it to get quite windy. This made our umbrellas less useful, particularly Phil's, as one of the arms snapped and poked a hole through the canopy. It also made it way colder. As our rain gear wetted out, and the wind picked up it became quite miserable.

The plan for the day was to make it to the next hut, where Phil would be finished, and I could hopefully dry out. But as the day went on, I was thinking more and more how nice it would be to not have to walk tomorrow. Just to lay all my stuff out and get it properly dry for sure. But that would require getting to Kapunda. Which would be an enormous day. Thought I'd just see how I was feeling at the hut.

There was a bit of road walking today, with the clumpy clau roads again

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034886.jpg" | relative_url }})

We headed towards Marabel, a little town on route. There's not much there, but there is a coffee shop. Unfortunately it's closed on Tuesday, the day we were there…

On the way there we passed the 700km marker. We were drenched by this point and knew if we stopped anywhere it wouldn't be long before we got super cold

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034887.jpg" | relative_url }})

Eventually we made it into Marabel. Apparently they're famous for a rodeo they hold annually.

We found a shelter near the playground and ate lunch there. It was only a few more kilometres for Phil to be finished completely.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034759.jpg" | relative_url }})

The rain was definitely starting to lessen up now. There were still drizzle here and there but most of it had died down. It also wasn't anywhere near as windy now.

Only a few minutes out of Marabel we hit the first flooded road. It had rained so much, and so consistently over the last 36 hours that roads had started to flood. The grass on both sides were completely flooded too. We can't go over it, we can't go under it, we're gonna have to go through it

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034888.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034767.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034774.jpg" | relative_url }})

At first, this was kinda cool. We were already soaked through, especially the shoes, so it wasn't really getting any more wet. But it quickly became a pattern… we ended up having to do 12 crossings like this. Some of them were up to my mid shin deep, and some were a good 20m long

Along this road, there were some cows on one side, and on the other was a bull. He was mooing so incredibly loud and consistently. We both were a bit nervous we could just take down the fence and charge us.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034778.jpg" | relative_url }})

We turned off those road onto a side “road”. This “road” has been fully flooded and was now a river flowing down hill.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034780.jpg" | relative_url }})

It was just after this that Phil was finished and his wife came to pick him up. I was on my own now and there was much more water ahead.

The hut, the original end point of today, was still another couple kms ahead.

I turned off the road, onto a canola paddock to head towards the hut and saw this

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034835.jpg" | relative_url }})

What I assume was a dam has over filled, and is flooding. That post with a white bit on top in the water is my trail marker…

I ended up routing the whole way around the same through the crop fields trying to keep my feet out of the mud.

Eventually I got through the farm and it was a little climb up to the hut. It was just past 12 by the time I made it there and I was planning to sit in there for a bit and have a breather. Maybe put my warm clothes on then decided to stay or go. When I got there, there was a sign saying it was 400m off trail to the hut. That meant another almost kilometre to go to the hut.

I decided on the spot to just send it the last 30km into Kapunda now. I was feeling pretty good, and the map made me think it was lots of roads which are pretty easy going.

From the hut is a nice ridge walk, except I had absolutely no views due to the fog

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034843.jpg" | relative_url }})

But literally 5 minutes later, the clouds parted for like 3 minutes and I got a view!

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034847.jpg" | relative_url }})

From this point, I was so focused on trying to make it to Kapunda in time to get a meal (everything is shut by 8:30) that I didn't really take many photos.

It was mostly just more of the same walking along roads or fences.

If you were watching the tracker closely, you may have noticed I went off the track and cut a corner. Half way along there, I realised I hadn't seen a marker for a while and I'd just been following someone's footprints. When I checked my GPS, which is preprogrammed the route into, it showed I was off track in the middle of nowhere.

No, no, no, no, no, no. I can't be off track. I dont really have that much time to waste if I want dinner.

When I pulled out my phone and looked at the actual map, it showed the reroute and I was so happy. Not only did it means I didn't have the backtrack, but it was a few ks shorter too.

As the reroute rejoins the main track, it crosses through a farm. Right in the front of the farm, there's a truck thats doing a side wheelie. I don't think trucks are meant to be like that

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034851.jpg" | relative_url }})

Eventually I made it to the last hill before Kapunda. In the distance I could just see the town, but I was pretty exhausted by this point, which you can probably see from this image.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-25/1000034866.jpg" | relative_url }})

The final stretch was just along the roads. I only needed my headlight for the final 45 minutes before I got into town and there were streetlights.

When I was close enough to get an estimate of when I'd be in town, I called the Pizza Bar and placed an order so I could just grab it and go straight to my cabin. I ended up making pretty good time, and got there just before 7.

I walked back to the caravan park, and crashed in my cabin. Ate the whole load of garlic bread and half the pizza family, then had a shower and came back and polished off the other half. It wasn't very good pizza, but I didn't really care at that point tbh.

I think it's pretty safe to say that my knee is not going to be holding me back anymore. It's still a little tender on the down hill sections, but nothing unmanageable.

It was a long walk. But the lure of a rest day and getting to properly dry everything out was an absolute drive. I'd walked longer distances in worse weather before, so knew it wasn't impossible.
