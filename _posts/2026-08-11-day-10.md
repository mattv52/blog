Total Falls

Phil 7 (+2) - Matt 4 (+1)

It was pretty hard to get up this morning, knowing we were heading back out and not going to have unlimited home cooked meals anymore.

With all the weather that happend yesterday, we are super happy we made the right decision. It wouldn't have been safe at all to be out in that weather, let alone in a tent.

Every time we pass a town with shop and resupply, my pack ends up heavier than it was last time. I think mine now weighs more than Phil's (it was ~14kg for me and 19kg for him at the start). I've lost just over a kilo in this first week, and my hiker hunger has started to set in, so I've packed a heap more food. Most will probably be eaten before Melrose.

We set out from Port Augusta nice and early to start a pretty big day back on the trail but on the way we got stuck. With all the rain we had this weekend, and particularly yesterday, lots of roads have been washed out or flooded. The road from Parachilna to Blinman (which is needed to get to the start of the trail) is currently flooded and not accessible. And our road into Quorn was flooded and up to half a metre deep.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034106.jpg" | relative_url }})

We weren't going to make it across in our Holden sedan. There wasn't much choice but to try hitch a ride in one of the big utes charging straight through. The first one that approached said "sure why not". Well that was easier than expected, and brings out hit rate up to 3/14 on getting rides.

They dropped us off in Quorn, but we finished our walk on Saturday a little bit further north of Quorn. So we set out to start there and connect the lines. But, as you may have seen on the tracker, we were met with another flooded road...

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034107.jpg" | relative_url }})

We decided it wouldn't be safe to cross, and thought to just start it from there.

The weather was actually not too bad at the start of the day. It was a bit windy, but the rain held off.

Once we left Quorn, and got on the dirt roads, it was quickly becoming very slippery. They were all wet and if not causing us to slip, it was clumping on our shoes making them way heavier. We decided to just jump the fence and walk ON the Pichi Richi rail tracks, instead of next to them

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034113.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034201.jpg" | relative_url }})

The trail left the tracks and went up into a small mountain to the side of the road which had a great view of the mountains we were about to head right into

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034202.jpg" | relative_url }})

When we started the slow ascent up into the mountains, it was along some more creek beds…. Except, this time they weren't dry. With all the rain we'd had they were flowing so much

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034203.jpg" | relative_url }})

We tried for so long to keep out feet wet. We ended up going up the side of hills just to avoid having to cross it. We lasted about 45 minutes before just giving up. At first Phil took his socks and shoes off and just wore his slides to get across, but later would join me and just send it in full socks and sneakers. The currents were so strong, not having our hiking poles to support us would have been worrying

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034128.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034204.jpg" | relative_url }})

At the deepest crossing it was up to our thighs, we had to roll up our shorts and take out phones out of our pockets. All up, there was 12 full wet crossings we had to take throughout the day.

Just before the end of the river section, both of us fell over trying to get down a hill and I ended up snapping one of my hiking poles. I need these to set up my tent. Luckily, we are in shelters the whole way to Melrose, where I'll get a replacement pair delivered.

Up until this point we had pretty good weather for walking. It was overcast, a little windy and hadn't rained. That all changed as we started to climb up Mount Brown. It is a long (5km) steady incline of about 500m elevation. It was brutal on it own, but we had it at the end of a long day, and the weather wasn't playing nice either. It also got pretty foggy with the rain

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034138.jpg" | relative_url }})

When we were almost at the top, we found an echidna sitting in the path, probably trying to stay warm

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034141.jpg" | relative_url }})

Along with the emus, kangaroos and wallabies, we are filling out our Australian animal bingo cards.

When we finally reached the very top, we were greeted with the amazing views…

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034144.jpg" | relative_url }})

…of nothing. It was pure whiteout in all directions. We couldn't hang around for long as the rain and wind were making us both very cold.

On the way down, the fog cleared for a few moments and so we got a small glimpse of a view

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034149.jpg" | relative_url }})

It's doesn't really come over in photos, but the way down was so steep. Being wet as well made for some slow going at points

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-10/1000034146.jpg" | relative_url }})

When we finally made it to camp, there was another guy there. After some chatting with him, we found out he was stuck in Quorn for 4 nights due to the weather!

Overall it was a pretty tough first day back. The weather was way better than we were expecting and very safe to hike in, until the summit push where it got a bit iffy.
