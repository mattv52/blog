After my friends dropped me off at the trail, and I rewalked the section back to grandpa's hut, I was rolling.

Today was I was heading through a couple conservation parks so I was expecting it to be quite nice, there were a couple hills that looked quite big though

Lot's of the day was walking through big trees providing lots of shade

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035070.jpg" | relative_url }})

These last couple days, where it's been the weekend and I've seen lots of people out for a casual stroll along the trail, I've had many people remark how lovely of a day it is for a walk. And don't get me wrong, it has been really nice weather, but I'm a hot sweaty guy who's not gonna be able to have a shower for a couple days, so cooler would be better in my opinion. And the shade provided by these trees do just that.

At the top of Montacute Conservation Park, there were 2 emus. I was quite surprised to see them this close to the city.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035080.jpg" | relative_url }})

Then it was a long walk downhill

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035082.jpg" | relative_url }})

At the bottom, there were a couple streams flowing which were quite picturesque

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035084.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035089.jpg" | relative_url }})

Eventually, I would have to go back up though. And the up was one of the steepest hills I've had to climb on trail so far. Easily top 3, and I've climbed a lot of hills.

Usually I try to keep my pace at the 4-5 km/h range when moving, which allows plenty of time for breaks as needed. Up his hill it was 1-2 km/h, to try give some context to how steep this hill is.

The picture doesn't do it justice at all, but trust me. It was steep

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035092.jpg" | relative_url }})

Up the top, when I finally got there, there was quite nice views the whole way around

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035093.jpg" | relative_url }})

There was a stretch of road walking along a semi main road, and I saw this sign. I thought it was waving at me for a second. Which probably shows where my head was at after that big hill in the heat

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035094.jpg" | relative_url }})

Just off the main road, Sarah, a friend from work, joined me for the rest of the day.

We headed off along a road for a bit, before reaching Morialta Conservation Park. A short stretch into it and I got my first glimpse of the city in almost a month

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035096.jpg" | relative_url }})

It's a bit of a weird feeling being so close to home now. Especially through here which are all trails that I've walked many times before.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035102.jpg" | relative_url }})

The trail goes along the Third Falls Hike route and visits all 3 falls. I realised while there that I don't think I've ever actually been to the third falls, usually just done the first 2 and turned around.

Just out of Morialta, up a short hill and we were at Norton Summit where my day was ending.

We hung out at the pub and a few more friends from work came up to join us for drinks/food there. (Plus one more who left before we took the picture)

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-30/1000035098.jpg" | relative_url }})

It was great to catch up with some familiar faces again, and to have someone to chat to while walking for a bit.

While up in the northern trail sections, everyone knows immediately that im on the Heysen when they see the backpack and asks about it. It seems like the closer I get to the city, the less people know and they just think I'm some crazy over prepared guy doing the 3 falls hike.

It was a pretty good day today. Any day that includes pub food is a good day tbh, but the added benefit of friends makes it better.

I was initially planning on staying at the hotel tonight, they don't have rooms but allow you to camp in the beer garden. But while there, it seemed like it wouldnt be that great a spot, and it was still very busy when I'd normally be getting ready to sleep.

So I decided to just keep walking along the trail and head into the next conservation park and just plonk my tent down somewhere. This is called stealth camping, and is technically not allowed on the Heysen, they really don't like you staying outside of designated camp sites, but I'll be packed up early and no one will have to know :) Except all of you who read this I guess, please don't dob me in?
