After spending the day after posting this walking, some more things came to mind that are probably relevant and worth telling. From now I plan to write updates each night and post at town, instead of trying to recall and cramming it in before bed so this will probably be the only part 2.

When we got to the start, there was another young couple there starting their walk. Also planning to do a thru hike in about the same time frame as I. Our first weeks matched up entirely so we would be spending a lot of time together in camp likely. Phil had a great chat with them at Aroona on the first night, and we were expecting to see them at Yanyanna Hut, but they never rolled in. We assumed they had changed plans and went to the earlier but instead. At Yanyanna LuckiePhil became UnluckiePhil, when he realised he lost his favourite headlamp. Fast forward to Wilpena Pound, Phil and I are wandering around looking for the shop, and we happened to walk past the cabin of the couple for the start (with his parents). She had a previous shoulder injury that they thought was fine, but flared up again on the second day so they bailed out and are planning to do lots of day walks while it recovers with his parents running taxi (we will see them again in the future back out on the trail). BUT they happened to walk past the bit of the trail that LuckiePhil dropped his headlamp before getting picked up and they returned it to him! LuckiePhil strikes again.

The other set of events I wanted to include are at Yanyanna Hut. We met a guy there who was super nice. After chatting for a bit, he asked if we would mind if he started a fire in the fire pit. Both Phil and I hadn't really considered it much before but had no objections. The huts in a bit of a clearing and there's not really any wood nearby. But after a few minutes, he wandered back from the river bed with a range of drift wood ready to burn. In a matter of seconds, he got it going. From then, we thought it'd be pretty nice to have fires, especially in some of those really cold nights. So thanks to him, we plan to have fires semi regularly when we aren't too tired at camp :)
