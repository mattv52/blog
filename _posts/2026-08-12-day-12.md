Total Falls

Phil 9 (+1) - Matt 5 (+1)

We woke up pretty early, knowing we had a 3.5km walk to get back to the trail before we started. We were still on the edge of if we would stay at Grays Hut at the base of Mount Remarkable and go over in the morning, of just send it all today. It was going to be based on what time we got there and how we felt.

Today there were more wet creek crossings. We managed to stay dry over the first couple crossings, but pretty quickly had to succumb to the water

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034211.jpg" | relative_url }})

Third day in a row of pretty much having wet feet all day. And creek water absolutely reeks. My feet, socks and shoes have all never smelt so bad.

By 7am we reached Stony Creek Camp Site. The guy we met at Catninga Shelter was camping here, and he also planned to go into Melrose today. He was still asleep in his tent as we went past, but is a fast walker and will catch up to us for sure.

The first hill of the day is always a big effort. And today it was a really big one

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034215.jpg" | relative_url }})

We saw our first deer today too. Drinking from the creek as we approached, then it zoomed off. It was the quickest acceleration I've ever seen up a hill. I guess it needs to be quick so it can escape predators, but wow. It made me kinda jealous how effortless it got up the hill we were slowly trudging up

Being spring, all the baby animals are out and about. Weve seen the emus and goats on previous days, today we saw a few Joey's in pouches

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034224.jpg" | relative_url }})

A little further along, we saw a couple of, presumably male, kangaroos having a fight. Like a full on fight. They were grappling with each other, and we could hear their double footed kicks cracking through the woods

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034233.jpg" | relative_url }})

Through the woods, we ended up following another creek in a valley between two hills. Except, it was no longer a dry creek bed. It was now a full flowing stream.

This made it quite difficult to navigate along as the path was seemingly underwater too. We ended up scaling the side of this hill so we could walk, but it was very rough terrain and our ankle were at 45 degrees pretty much the whole time. And with wet feet, this started to wear down on them and cause rubbing.

We missed our turnoff and we're on the exact opposite hill, so had to find some way to get down and across the stream to get back on trail, but it cost is a fair bit of time.

When we finally made it through and got to Grays Hut, the last but before Mount Remarkable, and our decision point of if we send it over today, we were both pretty knackered. Phil even had a little nap

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034236.jpg" | relative_url }})

While we were so tired, we had made such good time (arriving here just after 12), that even if it took us longer, it would still be worth going over today. Just think about the warm showers and Parmi on the other side.

We thought we were just going up from here, but there was a steep drop down into the valley, before coming back up the other side. It was pretty demoralising and the climb up was a big slog. At least there was a couple nice waterfalls we saw along the way (even though some were mostly hidden by trees)

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034300.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034302.jpg" | relative_url }})

When we finally reached the summit, we were expecting a nice viewing platform and a clearing to see the whole way around, similar to Mount Brown a couple days ago. There was no such luck…. It was just trees the whole way around. No view at all. We were pretty disappointed.

Then the climb down began. We knew it was a long descent, about 6.5km, but man it sucked. It was the most drawn out slow descent in history. Being so close to our accommodation, and it having been such a long tiring day probably didn't help, but it just felt like it kept going on and on and on. The descent was so small, tgat it kinda felt we were walking uphill sometimes…

Anyway, there were a few spots along the cliff that looks like this large rock slide. It's a pretty iconic spot, but a bit perilous. If you fell off the edge, it was a long way down

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034301.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-12/1000034303.jpg" | relative_url }})

After what felt like hours, we finally rolled into Melrose. I have hurt my knee a little on the walk down. Hopefully nothing too serious, just really not ideal to be walking on. So I decided it would be best to take a rest day here to let it recover. It also meant I wouldn't be in a rush to get going the next day after Phil left.

We stayed at the caravan park, which was the cheapest place we could find, and booked it for 2 nights.

All up, it was absolutely the hardest day we did. It wasn't the longest in time or distance, but it was in verticality, both up and down, by a fair bit. The stats up the top also don't include the hour and 4km we walked from our hotel to rejoin the trail.

This was always planned to be my first rest day, but then the weather rolled in and made Quorn make more sense. But since we pushed so hard, we are still 3 days ahead of Phil's original plan. So a rest day felt wise
