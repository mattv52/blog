It was a bit of a strange feeling waking up this morning knowing it was the final day. Usually I'm careful to try keep the inside of the tent dry and will pack things up in a particular way, but it didn't matter today.

We set off and immediately had a downhill, into an uphill. We we started to go down, Sam pointed out that we could see the trail going back up the hill on the other side already. If you look closely, you'll be able to see it slightly to the right of middle

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035428.jpg" | relative_url }})

Pretty much all day we walked through area where the fire had come. Here's one of the signs that was left standing with part of it burnt

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035431.jpg" | relative_url }})

Along all the valleys, there were heaps of lillys growing

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035432.jpg" | relative_url }})

Most of the trees had very few leaves left on them. These pineapple looking plants with the charred black trunks were sprouting these really tall stems. It was a really strange sight to see soooo many of them everywhere, they must be quite fire resilient

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035433.jpg" | relative_url }})

Most kangaroos early on the trail wouldnt let you get close. Maybe the ones down here are more familiar with people since these trails are much more popular, or maybe word spread that I'm a friendly guy in the kangaroo community?

Either way, this mum and Joey were quite happy to sit there as we walked past a couple metres away

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035439.jpg" | relative_url }})

The fresh light green moss on the ground and the charred black trees presented a pretty cool view. It almost looks a little radioactive

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035441.jpg" | relative_url }})

Eventually we reached the southernmost point, completing the quadfecta (if that's a word). As I expected, it wasn't at the southern trail head, but a bit before

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035450.jpg" | relative_url }})

Sam and I spent a good 20 minutes watching these 2 guys down in this bay trying to do something with fishing line and a bunch of balloons. One would stand on the beach at one side of the cove, and the other would go out on the rocks. They each had the fishing line in hand. The guy on the rocks would throw his end out, which had 4 balloons tied to it and the other would try pull it over to the middle.

We think there was probably a fishing hook or bait on the end of it, but they sucked sooo much at whatever they were trying to do. Lot's of yelling and trying again.

It was a nice beach though

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035458.jpg" | relative_url }})

The final stretch was past all the hills and it was just a couple small bumps through to the end. There was a section though a nice green tree area

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035463.jpg" | relative_url }})

Then lots of shrub land. For some reason this section had tree branches covering the ground like you might use bark in a playground

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035464.jpg" | relative_url }})

Towards the end, Phil, his wife and my mum all came out to meet me for the final couple kilometres. It got very windy again along the coast

Eventually, I made it to the end. There's not really a big gateway like there is for the start, just a small sign

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035539.jpg" | relative_url }})

So why'd the human cross the road?

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-36/1000035538.jpg" | relative_url }})

Oooh, this one I do know. Because the car to get home was on the other side :)

And that's a wrap
