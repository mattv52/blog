I decided to go for a long day to get into Spalding today, instead of the campsite half way. This meant a big 40+km day. But knowing the terrain was pretty easy, I thought it would be a good test of my knee. The hot shower and cooked meal were a massive bonus too (as long as it wasn't crystal brook all over again).

I set off nice and early, just as the sun was rising.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034423.jpg" | relative_url }})

The earlier I start, the earlier I can have a nice hot shower.

As the sun rose, the clouds started to look very worrying in the direction I was heading. A big roll of clouds, sweeping over the horizon

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034425.jpg" | relative_url }})

There was nothing to worry about in the end, no rain while walking again.

On a seperate note, why did the chicken cross the road?

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034427.jpg" | relative_url }})

Yea, I dunno either, but I saw it happen in Georgetown, a small community the trail passed through.

The trail goes on along the roads for another long while, which is all part of the re route around the new windmill farm I mentioned yesterday.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034432.jpg" | relative_url }})

While walking along the roads, a helicopter flew by, quite low. It looked to be a surf rescue chopper, with the yellow and red paint job. I looked it up on flight radar, and it didn't show up. Pretty strange.

Eventually, I made it past the rerouted section and back onto the original trail.

It was just through some farmland, but the terrain was way more interesting.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034448.jpg" | relative_url }})

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034451.jpg" | relative_url }})

I stopped and refilled one of my bottles of water at the camp. I would probably have been fine if I didn't, but I'd drunk a lot more than I expected and was running low.

There's a creek running through the property that the trail goes alongside. The creek is full of reeds. It was quite mesmerising watching them blow in the breeze

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034452.jpg" | relative_url }})

At the end of the farmland, I could see what looked like a toilet building. But it was the middle of nowhere, and nothing was marked on my map. Turned out the farmland ended right as Bundaleer Reservoir began.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034456.jpg" | relative_url }})

The next section was along an aquaduct, and went for ages. Since this section is shared with the Mawson Trail (which is a similar trail, but for cyclists, not walkers) all of the fence crossings in this section were gates, not stiles, and they came in pairs. They got very old quick, especially towards the end of the day.

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034465.jpg" | relative_url }})

On what turned out to be the 4th to last gate (of probably 25-30 today), I thought the fence looked low enough to just step over with my long legs. I was wrong…

![]({{ "/assets/images/day-18/1000034660.jpg" | relative_url }})

It's not too bad, and should be pretty easy to repair when I get a chance, but is really annoying.

Finally I rolled into Spalding, keen to just crash. My mate that I've been with for the last couple nights beat me in and had a suspicious look on his face.

There were no rooms at the hotel... None. Not one. The only hope was 1 booking wasn't fully confirmed yet. So we waited around, finally the hotel guy came back out and said he hadn't heard anything but it was a hiker who was planning to be here by now. When my mate heard his name, he immediately knew they weren't close cause he was hiking with his brother (about a week behind us). Luckily that room was a family room and he was happy to split it with me.

The Spalding hotel was also "shut" for dinner, but he opened up the kitchen for us all staying there which was excellent.

We later found out it was booked up by all the tradies working on the windmill farm. Apparently they booked every room in Spalding. Wow

It was a long day, but pretty easy walking. My knee held up great, no issues at all. Which is super promising for the rest of the trail. Tomorrow is another huge day, even longer, and much tougher terrain.
